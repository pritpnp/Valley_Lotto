What do these four things mean?

The score out of 100 combines the four things below into one answer. At or above the keep line, the game is worth its box. Below it, the game is doing worse for you than the space it takes up. Every number is one Pennsylvania publishes; nothing is guessed.

Prizes left (counts the most). Out of the prizes Pennsylvania reports for this game, how many haven't been won yet, leaving the top prize out. A new game is near 100%. A game down at 20% has had most of its good tickets taken. Pennsylvania only reports the bigger prize levels, so the smallest wins ($1, $2, $5…) aren't in this count.

Wins more than it costs. How often a ticket pays back more than it cost, from the full list of prizes the game was printed with, small ones included. This is what keeps a regular playing. It's compared with the best game at the same price, since a $1 and a $30 game can't be compared.

Top prizes left. How many of the top prizes are still out there. It counts, but less than the rest: a game can lose its jackpot and still pay out well.

Wins anything. How often a ticket wins at all, even just the price of the ticket back. Set when the game is printed, and never changes. Also compared with the best game at the same price.

Where a part says left out, Pennsylvania's numbers for it couldn't be checked, and the other parts share its weight. A missing piece of information never counts against a game.
