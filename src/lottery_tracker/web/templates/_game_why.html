How is this worked out?

You earn 5% of every ticket you sell, and the lottery pays back what you pay out. So a game earns its box by selling. The score out of 100 is how well this game sells compared with the typical game at the same price: 100 means it sells at least as well as the typical one, 20 means a fifth as well. Under {{ '%.0f'|format(detail.cutoff or 20) }}, send it back.

Where the sales come from. Pennsylvania doesn't publish sales, but about once a week it posts how many prizes of each game are still unclaimed. Prizes are spread evenly through every game, so as tickets sell, prizes get claimed at the same rate. From the last five weeks of those counts: prizes claimed a day ÷ prizes printed = the share of the game sold a day. Times the tickets printed and the price, that's dollars of tickets sold a day across the state. The "give or take" is how far the weekly counts stray from a steady line.

Your own counts join in. Once your store has two weeks of morning and night counts, a game you carry is also rated on what it sells in your store (dollars a box a day) against your own typical box at the same price, and the score is the average of the two: half Pennsylvania, half your store.

Compared at the same price only. A $1 game and a $30 game can't be compared, so each game is measured against the middle game of those on sale at its price.

The other facts (prizes left, how often it wins more than it costs, top prizes left, how often it wins anything) are shown so you can see what the game offers. They don't change the score: whatever customers make of them already shows in what they buy.

When a game's sales can't be measured yet, usually because it's too new for three weekly counts, it says so and stays. Nothing is guessed.
