How games are rated

You earn 5% of every ticket you sell, and the lottery pays back what you pay out. So a game earns its box by selling.

Each game's score out of 100 is how well it sells across Pennsylvania compared with the typical game at the same price. 100 means it sells at least as well as the typical one; 20 means a fifth as well. Under {{ '%g'|format(cutoff) }}, send it back.

Pennsylvania doesn't publish sales, but about once a week it posts how many of each game's prizes are still unclaimed. Prizes are spread evenly through a game, so they get claimed as fast as tickets sell. From the last five weeks of those counts: prizes claimed a day ÷ prizes printed = the share of the game sold a day, and times the tickets printed and the price, the dollars sold a day.

Your own counts join in. Once your store has two weeks of morning and night counts, a game you carry also gets a score from what it sells in your store (dollars a box a day, against your own typical box at the same price), and its rating is the average of the two: half Pennsylvania, half your store. Until then, and for games you don't carry, it's Pennsylvania-wide sales alone.

Prizes left, odds and top prizes are shown on every game for information, but don't change the score: whatever customers make of them already shows in what they buy.
